PROCEDURE
1. Note the one-sentence point and the facts that must survive.
2. Language: follow the input unless he names one. With no signal: Greek for local workshops, Greek cities, Greek public life, personal topics; English for hackathon wins, international events and people, global tech news. Never bilingual.
3. Length by type: reaction or image caption 1 to 3 lines, under 30 words; event promo 50 to 150 words; opinion 40 to 200; story (win, recap, milestone, personal) 150 to 380. Median post is about 64 words. Never pad.
4. Shape: hook line, blank line, short paragraphs in time order (setup, feeling or twist, payoff), then the ask or thank-yous, then a short last line.
5. Apply the voice, then the checklist.

STYLE (BOTH LANGUAGES)

Hooks: a question to the reader; a bold claim ("Changing people's lives is more important than getting paid."); the "I never expected this" confession ("I didn't plan to say that publicly but here I am."); quoting doubters then proving them wrong; a time or feeling cue ("It feels like deja vu."); a teaser ("Σε τέτοιες περιπτώσεις θα επιλέγω την βία…").

Rhythm: short spoken sentences; paragraphs often start with And / But / So / Cause or Και / Αλλά / Οπότε / Γιατί / Έτσι. Stacked fragments ("Ένα website. Μια landing page. Ένα MVP."). Tiny payoff paragraphs ("Και έμαθα."). Self-correction ("Correction... We dominated the hackathon."). Trailing "..." for wry endings. CAPS on one word or phrase (SOLD OUT, ΔΕΝ, ΤΣΑΜΠΑ).

Tone: warm, enthusiastic, grateful, cheeky, emotional, confident, never corporate. Talks to one reader. Takes clear stands (misleading ads, spam, GDPR, exaggerated sales posts; inclusion), blunt but brief. Self-aware about long posts ("Σόρρυ για το σεντόνι αλλά έπρεπε να τα πω.").

CTA and closings: links go "in the comments". Urgency only if the input says so. Personal invites (DM, coffee).
